Stages are the columns of your board, and they are the board's own — a stage exists on one pipeline
and nowhere else. Manage them under **Lead stages**, at the top of **Pipeline settings**.

<ScreenShot src="/docs/screenshots/pipelines/pipelines-stages.png" alt="The lead stages table in pipeline settings" />

---

## What a Stage Carries

| Field | Does |
|-------|------|
| **Colour** | The left edge of every card in the column, and the dot beside its name. Picked from a fixed palette |
| **Lead stage** | Its name, everywhere on the board. Blanking the field reverts it |
| **Status** | **Open**, **Won** or **Lost** — what landing here does to a lead |
| **Probability** | 0–100. Weights the [Forecast](/pipeline/forecast) and shows as *"{`{n}`}% likely"* on the lead's stage stepper |
| **Rotting after** | Days of silence before a lead here is flagged. **0** means never |

The row also shows how many **open** leads the column holds — a won or lost lead sitting in it is
not counted, the same way the board leaves closing columns out of its open totals. Number and text
fields save when you click away from them; the colour and status selects save immediately.

<Callout type="warn">
  Status is the one field that changes behaviour rather than presentation. Switching an ordinary
  column to **Won** or **Lost** makes every future drop onto it close a lead — and leaves the leads
  already sitting there untouched, so the column's status and its contents can disagree until they
  are moved.
</Callout>

## What a New Board Starts With

Creating a pipeline gives you seven columns and five lost reasons. All of them are editable, and
none of them are special.

| Stage | Status | Probability | Rotting after |
|-------|--------|-------------|---------------|
| **New Lead** | Open | 10% | 3 days |
| **Contacted** | Open | 25% | 4 days |
| **Qualified** | Open | 45% | 5 days |
| **Demo / Proposal** | Open | 65% | 7 days |
| **Negotiation** | Open | 80% | 5 days |
| **Won** | Won | 100% | — |
| **Lost** | Lost | 0% | — |

An organisation that had lifecycle stages before the pipeline shipped gets a board whose columns
carry those names instead, sorted into open, then won, then lost.

## Adding and Ordering

<Steps>
<Step>
**Add lead stage** appends a column called **New stage** at 50% and a 5-day rotting window. Rename
it in place straight away — the name is stored, so the whole organisation sees whatever you leave there.
</Step>
<Step>
Set its **Status**. This is the field to get right: a new stage defaults to **Open**.
</Step>
<Step>
Reorder by dragging a row's grip handle, or by focusing the handle and pressing the arrow keys. The
number on the left is the column's position, left to right on the board.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Callout type="info">
  A one-column board offers no handles and no delete — there is nothing to reorder, and a pipeline
  must keep at least one stage. The delete button's tooltip says so: *"A pipeline needs at least one
  lead stage"*.
</Callout>

---

## Deleting a Stage

Deleting asks where the leads in it should go: **Move its leads to** a column you pick. Nothing is
deleted with the stage, and the leads keep their notes, timelines and assignees — they simply land
somewhere else.

---

## Lost Reasons

Under the stages table, **Lost reasons** is the list your team picks from when marking a lead lost.
Each row shows how many leads have been attributed to it. **Add reason** appends one; the bin
removes it.

A new board starts with **Price too high**, **No response**, **Chose a competitor**,
**Not the right fit** and **Timing — revisit later**.

<Callout type="warn">
  Deleting a reason is not blocked by the leads already using it, and the count beside it is your
  only warning. Rename a reason you have outgrown rather than deleting it, or its share of your
  historical losses becomes unattributable.
</Callout>

---

## Choosing Windows That Work

- **Match the window to the step, not the board.** A first reply is late after two days; a proposal
  under review is not late after two weeks.
- **Leave closing columns at zero.** Nothing in them can be late — the lead is already done.
- **Set probabilities that step up.** They drive the forecast, and a board where three consecutive
  stages all read 50% forecasts nothing useful.
- **Fewer columns than you think.** Every column is a decision someone has to make about which one a
  lead belongs in.
