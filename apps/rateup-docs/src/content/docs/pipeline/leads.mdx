Everything about one lead lives in a drawer that slides in from the right. Open it by clicking a
card on the [Board](/pipeline/board), or a row in the [List](/pipeline/list).

## Adding a Lead

**Add lead** in the header opens on no particular column; the **+** on a column header opens on that
one.

<Steps>
<Step>
Pick the **Customer**, either way round. **Choose from contacts** searches your existing
[contacts](/contacts) by name, phone or email; **Enter details manually** takes a **Contact name**,
an optional **Business / company** and an optional **WhatsApp number** for somebody who is not in
your contacts yet.
</Step>
<Step>
Pick the **Stage** it starts in.
</Step>
<Step>
Pick the **Source** — **WhatsApp enquiry**, **Instagram DM**, **Messenger**, **Web survey**,
**Referral program**, **Loyalty program** or **Added manually**. The first three also set the
channel the lead is reachable on.
</Step>
<Step>
Optionally tap the **Assignees** you want on it, then **Add lead**.
</Step>
</Steps>

The lead copies the contact's name, phone and email so a card can render without a second lookup,
but it is the contact link that matters — it is what makes **Open conversation** work.

A number typed in manually is looked up as you finish it, and the line under the field says what
will happen with it:

| It says | Meaning |
|---------|---------|
| *Nobody has this number yet — it will be added to your contacts* | A new contact is created with the lead |
| *{`{name}`} already has this number — the lead will be linked to them* | **Use their details** links the lead to that contact instead of duplicating them |
| *Linked to {`{name}`} — the lead opens their conversation in Inbox* | Already linked. **Unlink** breaks it |

A name on its own is enough to save. That lead has no contact behind it, so its card carries the
broken-link icon and **Open conversation** stays disabled until somebody gives it a number.
A number that is typed has to be dialable — *"That number doesn't look right."* refuses anything
else, since the lead carries it verbatim.

<Callout type="info">
  Choosing a closing column says so: *"{`{stage}`} is a closing column — the lead opens already
  closed."* That is occasionally what you want, for a deal you are recording after the fact.
</Callout>

## The Header

| Button | Does |
|--------|------|
| **Open conversation** | Jumps to this contact in the [Inbox](/inbox). Disabled on a lead with no contact |
| **Won** | Opens the close confirmation. Disabled on a lead already won |
| **Lost** | The same, asking for a reason. Disabled on a lead already lost |
| **⋯ → Reopen lead** | Puts a closed lead back on the board. Disabled while it is open |
| **⋯ → Delete lead** | Removes the lead outright |

<Callout type="warn">
  **Delete lead** asks nothing. The lead, its notes, its activities and its whole timeline go
  immediately, and the drawer closes. If what you want is to record that it did not work out, use
  **Lost** — a lost lead stays on the board and still counts in your [insights](/pipeline/insights).
</Callout>

Under the buttons sits the **stage stepper**: every column in order, the current one highlighted,
with its win probability and how many days the lead has been in it. Clicking another step moves the
lead — including onto a closing column, which asks the same way a drag does.

## The Next Activity

The single most useful field on a lead: what happens next, and when.

<Steps>
<Step>
**Schedule** opens the panel. Type what has to happen — *"Follow up on the quote"*.
</Step>
<Step>
Pick a **Type**: **Call**, **Message**, **Meeting**, **Document** or **To-do**. The type only
chooses the icon that shows on the card.
</Step>
<Step>
Set a **Due** date and time, and optionally who it is assigned to. Then **Schedule**.
</Step>
</Steps>

Once scheduled, the panel offers **Mark done**. An overdue activity turns red on the card and in the
list. A lead with nothing scheduled shows **No next activity scheduled** in amber, and appears under
the sidebar's **No next activity** view.

<Callout type="info">
  *Leads without a next step stall.* The **No next activity** count is the one number on this screen
  you can act on immediately — every lead in it is a decision nobody has made.
</Callout>

## Details

| Field | Notes |
|-------|-------|
| **Assignees** | Everyone working the lead. Pick from the people on the board's teams; **Unassign everyone** clears it |
| **Owner** | Whoever opened it. Fixed, and shown as **Added automatically** for a lead a rule opened |
| **Team** | The team it belongs to, or **No team** |
| **Source** and **Channel** | How it arrived, and where the customer is reachable |
| **Lead age** | Days since it was created |
| **Idle** | Days since anything happened. Reads *"{`{n}`} days — stalled"* once past the stage's window |
| **Expected close** | Feeds the [Forecast](/pipeline/forecast). **Not set** puts it in the unscheduled bucket |
| **Contact** | The linked contact, or **Not linked** |

### Labels

Labels are the board's own, defined per pipeline, and are separate from
[contact labels](/contacts/labels). The drawer's **Add** button puts an existing label on the lead,
and the chips also show on its board card.

<Callout type="warn">
  A board with no labels reads *"This pipeline has no labels yet — add them in pipeline settings"* —
  but the current build has no label editor in pipeline settings, and the pipeline is not part of the
  [external API](/external-api) either. Until an editor ships there is no way to create one, so the
  picker will stay empty. Nothing else on the lead depends on labels.
</Callout>

## Timeline and Notes

**Timeline** is the automatic record — created, moved, assignees changed, won, lost, reopened, note
added, activity scheduled and completed. Each entry names who did it, or says *"Added by automation"*
when a rule did.

**Notes** is what your team writes down. A note is attributed and timestamped, and can be deleted by
anyone who can open the lead.

<Callout type="info">
  Notes count as activity, so leaving one resets the idle clock and clears a **needs attention**
  flag. That is intended — someone has looked at it — but it does mean a lead can be kept out of the
  stalled list without moving forward.
</Callout>

## Closing a Lead

Closing happens three ways, and all three end in the same confirmation: **Won** or **Lost** in the
drawer, the stage stepper, or dragging the card onto a closing column.

| Outcome | What it asks |
|---------|--------------|
| **Won** | Confirmation only. *"The lead moves to the won column and stops counting as open."* |
| **Lost** | A **Lost reason**, picked from the board's list — and it is required when the board has any |

Both accept an optional closing note, which is kept on the timeline.

<Callout type="warn">
  The lost reason is the only input the *"why do we lose deals"* side of reporting ever gets. A board
  with no lost reasons configured lets a loss through without one, and that loss can never be
  attributed afterwards — add them in [Settings](/pipeline/settings) before your team starts closing.
</Callout>

A closed lead stays on the board in its closing column, stops counting as open, and can be put back
with **Reopen lead**.

## Troubleshooting

| Symptom | Cause | Fix |
|---------|-------|-----|
| **Open conversation** is greyed out | The lead has no linked contact | It was created without one; raise a new lead from the contact |
| Nobody appears in the assignee picker | Nobody is on the board's teams yet | Add a team in [Settings](/pipeline/settings) |
| **Mark lost** stays disabled | The board has lost reasons but none is selected | Pick one |
| The lead has no labels to choose | The board has none defined | Add them in pipeline settings |
