**Forecast** answers one question: how much is likely to land, and when. It groups your open leads
by their **expected close** month and weights each one by the win probability of the stage it is in.

<ScreenShot src="/docs/screenshots/pipelines/pipeline-forcast.png" alt="The Forecast view, with every open lead in the Unscheduled column" />

---

## Reading a Column

| Element | Meaning |
|---------|---------|
| The month | Formatted from each lead's **Expected close** date |
| **{`{n}`} leads** | How many open leads fall in that month |
| The big number | **Expected to convert** — the probability-weighted count |
| The bar | That number against the strongest month showing |
| Each row | A lead, with its stage and that stage's probability. Click to open it |

The headline number is deliberately not the raw lead count. Five leads sitting at 20% are one
expected win, and the column says **1** rather than **5**.

---

## The Unscheduled Column

Open leads with no **Expected close** date collect in a trailing **Unscheduled** column rather than
being dropped from the view.

<Callout type="warn">
  An unscheduled lead is invisible to your forecast in every other respect, so the column is
  usually the most important one on this screen. If it is the tallest column you have, the forecast
  is not measuring your pipeline — it is measuring the part of it somebody remembered to date.
</Callout>

Set a date from the lead drawer's **Expected close** field — see
[Working a Lead](/pipeline/leads#details).

---

## What It Includes

Only **open** leads. Won and lost leads have already landed, so they belong in
[Insights](/pipeline/insights) rather than a forecast.

Everything else the board is filtering on still applies — a search, an assignee, a team or a source
facet narrows the forecast the same way it narrows the board. That makes *"what is Priya expected to
close this quarter"* a two-click question.
