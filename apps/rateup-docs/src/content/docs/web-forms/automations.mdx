## Google Review Routing

Send satisfied respondents straight to your Google review page, and unsatisfied ones somewhere
useful instead.

<ScreenShot src="/docs/screenshots/web-forms/google-review.png" alt="Google review routing" />

<Steps>
<Step>
Add a **Google Review** question, or configure **Google Review Redirects**.
</Step>
<Step>
Enter the **condition and Google page URL** — which answers qualify, and where to send them.
</Step>
<Step>
Save. Qualifying respondents see the review prompt; everyone else continues normally.
</Step>
</Steps>

You can also route to other platforms — **Facebook**, **Instagram**, **Amazon**, **MakeMyTrip**, or
a custom platform with **Enter Custom Platform Name**.

<Callout type="warn">
  Route on the answer, not on everyone. Asking an unhappy customer for a public review is how a
  private complaint becomes a one-star rating. The condition is the whole point of this feature.
</Callout>

### The pattern that works

```
"How was your experience?"
   ├── Great / Excellent  →  Google review prompt
   └── Poor / Okay        →  "What went wrong?" → ticket
```

Happy customers get a frictionless path to saying so in public. Unhappy ones get a path to being
heard in private — and a chance for you to fix it before they post anyway.

## Automatic Ticket Creation

**Enable Auto-Ticket Creation** turns qualifying responses into tickets on a
[board](/tickets/boards), so nothing gets lost in a spreadsheet.

<Steps>
<Step>
Enable **Auto-Ticket** on the form or the question.
</Step>
<Step>
**Choose a board** and **choose a column** for new tickets to land in.
</Step>
<Step>
Set the **condition for ticket issue** — which answers should raise one.
</Step>
<Step>
**Choose the teams to be added** so the right people see it.
</Step>
</Steps>

### What to trigger on

| Trigger | Why |
|---------|-----|
| Detractor NPS scores | Every one is a customer worth rescuing |
| A complaint keyword in free text | Catches problems the rating missed |
| A specific choice — *"Report a problem"* | Explicit, unambiguous |

<Callout type="info">
  Do not raise a ticket for every response. A board full of "everything was fine" cards is a board
  your team stops reading — and then they miss the one that mattered.
</Callout>

## Closing the Loop

The full sequence, when both automations are set up:

1. A customer submits a poor rating
2. A ticket is created on your support board, assigned to the right team
3. An agent opens it, sees the response, and contacts the customer
4. The problem is fixed and the ticket closed
5. Next time, that customer is a promoter — and the review prompt is the right thing to show them

That loop is the difference between measuring satisfaction and improving it.
