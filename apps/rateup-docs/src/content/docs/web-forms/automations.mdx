Review routing turns a form from a data-collection exercise into something that acts on what it
learns: it decides, per answer, who gets asked for a public review and who gets handled privately
instead.

---

## Google Review Routing

Send satisfied respondents straight to your Google review page, and unsatisfied ones somewhere
useful instead.

<ScreenShot src="/docs/screenshots/web-forms/google-review.png" alt="Google review routing" />

<Steps>
<Step>
Add a **Google Review** question, or configure **Google Review Redirects**.
</Step>
<Step>
Enter the **condition and Google page URL** — which answers qualify, and where to send them.
</Step>
<Step>
Save. Qualifying respondents see the review prompt; everyone else continues normally.
</Step>
</Steps>

You can also route to other platforms — **Facebook**, **Instagram**, **Amazon**, **MakeMyTrip**, or
a custom platform with **Enter Custom Platform Name**.

<Callout type="warn">
  Route on the answer, not on everyone. Asking an unhappy customer for a public review is how a
  private complaint becomes a one-star rating. The condition is the whole point of this feature.
</Callout>

### The pattern that works

```
"How was your experience?"
   ├── Great / Excellent  →  Google review prompt
   └── Poor / Okay        →  "What went wrong?" → follow up privately
```

Happy customers get a frictionless path to saying so in public. Unhappy ones get a path to being
heard in private — and a chance for you to fix it before they post anyway.

## Following Up on a Poor Response

Forms no longer raise tickets on a board — RateUp has no ticket boards. Act on a poor response
where the customer already is:

| Route | How |
|-------|-----|
| The conversation | A form sent over WhatsApp files its answers against the contact's conversation, so the agent replying already sees them — see [Results](/web-forms/results) |
| An automation rule | A [Send Web Survey](/automation/actions) rule can be paired with an **Assign Team** rule so poor responses land with the right people |
| The sales board | Put the contact on a [pipeline](/pipeline) stage when the follow-up is a deal rather than a complaint |
| Email | **Enable Notification Email** in [Settings](/web-forms/settings) mails each submission to an address you choose |

<Callout type="info">
  Do not route every response to a person. A queue full of "everything was fine" is a queue your
  team stops reading — and then they miss the one that mattered.
</Callout>

---

## Closing the Loop

The full sequence, when review routing is set up:

1. A customer submits a poor rating, and is not shown the review prompt
2. Their answers land against their conversation in the [Inbox](/inbox)
3. An agent opens it, sees the response, and replies to the customer
4. The problem is fixed and the conversation closed
5. Next time, that customer is a promoter — and the review prompt is the right thing to show them

That loop is the difference between measuring satisfaction and improving it.
