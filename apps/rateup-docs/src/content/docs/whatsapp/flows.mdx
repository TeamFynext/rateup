A **flow** is a form built from screens that a customer fills in inside WhatsApp — no browser, no
link, no app switch.

Use flows for anything structured: a booking, a feedback survey, a lead form, an order detail.

## Creating a Flow

<Steps>
<Step>
Click **Create Flow**. The dialog starts with **What for** — *"What is this flow for?"*
</Step>
<Step>
Pick a purpose: **Sign up**, **Sign in**, **Appointment booking**, **Lead generation**, **Contact
us**, **Customer support**, **Survey**, **Loyalty feedback** or **Other**. The purpose becomes the
flow's category, so there is nothing to choose later.
</Step>
<Step>
For **Loyalty feedback** only, a **Template** step follows: pick a ready-made feedback flow. One is
marked **Suggested for you**.
</Step>
<Step>
On **Details**, enter the **Name**, choose the **WhatsApp number** and **Select Teams** (at least one).
Leave the number blank and it is created on your first connected number. The number fixes the
business account the flow lives under — see below.
</Step>
<Step>
Click **Create Flow**, then build the screens in the [flow editor](/whatsapp/flow-editor).
</Step>
<Step>
Click **Save to WhatsApp** to push it live.
</Step>
</Steps>

## Read the Flow Cards

Each flow is a card:

| Part | Shows |
|------|-------|
| Subtitle | The flow's purpose — *Sign up*, *Survey*… — or *Flow* for older flows |
| Big number | How many responses it has collected |
| Team line | The teams the flow belongs to |
| Status badge | **Active**, **Draft**, **Deprecated**, **Throttled** or **Blocked** — Meta's state for the flow |

Clicking an **Active** flow opens it on its **Responses** tab; any other opens the editor. The card's
menu has **Clone**, **Copy & Renew Flow** (published flows only) and **Delete** — a draft is deleted,
a published flow is deprecated instead.

Use **Search flows...** and **Filter by Teams** to narrow the list once you have several.

## A Flow Belongs to One Business Account

You pick a **WhatsApp number** when you create a flow, but WhatsApp stores the flow against that
number's **WhatsApp Business Account** — the WABA the number sits under. That account, not the
number, is the boundary:

- **Numbers on the same business account share their flows.** A flow created on one can be sent from
  any of the others, and attached to a template built on any of them.
- **Nothing crosses to a second business account.** If you have connected numbers under two WABAs,
  neither one's flows are available to the other.

| Where the boundary applies | What happens |
|----------------------------|--------------|
| Sending a flow from a number on another business account | Refused before it reaches WhatsApp |
| Saving a template whose flow is on another business account | Rejected, naming the flow — see [Template Editor](/whatsapp/template-editor) |

The flows list shows every flow you have, across all numbers and accounts. It is not filtered, so the
business account matters when you attach or send a flow, not when you browse.

<Callout type="warn">
  The **Flow** picker on a template's button is stricter than the rule above: it lists flows recorded
  against **that exact number** only. A flow on a sibling number of the same business account will
  not appear, even though the template would be accepted if you could select it. Build the template
  on the number the flow was created on.
</Callout>

<Callout type="info">
  Flows created before the number was recorded belong to your **first connected number**, and so to
  its business account. If you run several numbers and an older flow is missing from the picker,
  that is usually why.
</Callout>

To use the same form under a second business account, build a separate flow there. **Copy & Renew
Flow** does not help — a copy is created on the same number as the original.

## Designing Screens That Get Finished

Flows have a much higher completion rate than web forms — but only if they are short.

- **One idea per screen.** Customers on a phone abandon dense screens.
- **Ask only what you will use.** Every extra field costs completions.
- **Put the easy questions first.** Momentum matters.
- **Make optional fields obviously optional.**

<Callout type="info">
  If your flow needs more than four or five screens, it is probably two flows — or a job for a
  conversation with an agent.
</Callout>

## Sending a Flow

A flow reaches customers in three ways:

| Route | Use |
|-------|-----|
| **From a conversation** | An agent sends it from the Inbox composer |
| **From a template** | Attach a flow button to an approved template |
| **From a chatbot** | A bot step opens the flow |

## Responses

Answers come back attached to the customer's conversation, so the agent handling the ticket sees
what was submitted without leaving the thread.

To push submissions into your own systems, set a **webhook** on the flow's Settings tab. It uses the
same signed, retried mechanism as web forms — see
[Web Forms → Settings](/web-forms/settings) for the payload, headers and verification.

To receive every Flow your organisation runs at one endpoint instead, subscribe the organisation
webhook to **Flow response created** (`flow.response.created`) under
[Settings → Integrations → Webhook](/settings/integrations#webhook). It carries the flow's name and
ids along with the answers.

## Syncing

Flows live on Meta's side too. Use **Sync** to align RateUp with WhatsApp after making changes.
It is disabled until a WhatsApp number is connected — *"Connect a WhatsApp number in settings to sync
a Flow"*.

<Callout type="warn">
  A flow that is edited but not synced still serves the old version to customers. Sync after every
  change you intend to be live.
</Callout>
