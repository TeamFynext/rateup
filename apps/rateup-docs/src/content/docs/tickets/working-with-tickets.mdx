## Creating a Ticket

<Steps>
<Step>
Open the board and add a ticket to a **column**.
</Step>
<Step>
Give it a title. A ticket left as *"Untitled Ticket"* will be ignored by everyone.
</Step>
<Step>
Add a **description** — click **Add a description…** and explain what needs doing and why.
</Step>
</Steps>

---

## The Fields

| Field | Purpose |
|-------|---------|
| **Assignee** | Who is doing it. **Unassigned** means nobody is |
| **Teams** | Which teams the ticket belongs to |
| **Column** | The stage it has reached |
| **Labels** | Board labels for severity, area or type |
| **Due date** | When it needs to be done. Late tickets show **Overdue** |
| **Customer** | The contact it relates to — search and attach one |
| **Created / Created by** | Recorded automatically |

## Checklists

Checklists break a ticket into steps that can be ticked off.

<Steps>
<Step>
Click **Add checklist** and name it.
</Step>
<Step>
Use **Add an item** for each step.
</Step>
<Step>
Tick items as they are completed.
</Step>
</Steps>

Use them when a ticket has a known sequence — a release checklist, a refund approval path. If you
have not added one yet you will see *"No checklists yet — add one above"*.

---

## Comments

Comments are the discussion thread on the ticket. Use **Add a comment…** to record decisions,
blockers and updates.

<Callout type="info">
  Put the *why* in comments. The description says what needs doing; comments are where the reasoning
  lives, and that is what makes an old ticket readable.
</Callout>

---

## Attachments

Attach screenshots, logs, documents or anything else the work needs. Use **Delete Attachment** to
remove one.

---

## Activity

The **Activity** feed records what happened to the ticket — moves, assignments, field changes.
Before it has any history it reads *"No activity yet."*

Use it to answer "when did this stall, and on whom?"

---

## Linking Back to the Inbox

Tickets raised from a customer conversation carry a **Customer**, and
**Open Chat In Inbox** takes you straight to that conversation.

This is the loop that makes board tickets worth keeping: the developer fixing the bug can read
exactly what the customer said, and the agent can tell the customer it is fixed.

---

## Working a Board Well

- Move cards as work progresses, not in a weekly tidy-up
- Assign someone — an unassigned ticket is nobody's
- Set due dates only when they mean something; **Overdue** stops mattering if everything is
- Close the loop with the customer when a ticket that came from a conversation is done
- Delete duplicates rather than letting two cards drift apart
