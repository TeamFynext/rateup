A **campaign** sends an approved [template](/whatsapp/templates) to a set of contacts. It is how
you start conversations at scale rather than one at a time.

<ScreenShot src="/docs/screenshots/campaign/list.png" alt="Your campaigns" />

---

## Two Kinds of Campaign

| Type | Sends |
|------|-------|
| **Broadcast** | One message to everyone, once |
| **Drip** | A sequence over time, paced by a daily send limit |

Broadcasts suit announcements and offers. Drips suit onboarding sequences and gradual re-engagement
— and they are gentler on your account's quality rating.

## Campaign Statuses

| Status | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| **Draft** | Being built, not sent |
| **Scheduled** | Queued for a future time |
| **Active** | Currently sending |
| **Completed** | Finished |
| **Inactive** | Paused or stopped |

## Before You Send

<Callout type="warn">
  Campaigns are the fastest way to damage a WhatsApp account. Sending an irrelevant message to a
  large list produces blocks and reports, which lower your quality rating and reduce how much you
  may send afterwards — a cost that outlasts the campaign by months.
</Callout>

Check all four:

1. The **template is approved** — unapproved templates cannot send
2. The audience has given **consent**
3. The list is **segmented** — relevance is what stops people blocking you
4. The variables are mapped correctly — test on yourself first
