**Spam** holds the contacts you have flagged as junk — promotional blasts, automated noise, and
anyone who is not a genuine customer.

Spam is a flag on the **contact**, not a status on a ticket. Flag someone once and they leave every
working view, however many tickets they have.

<Callout type="info">
  This is the one tab that lists **contacts** rather than tickets — one row per spam contact, and the
  row count reads *"12 contacts"* instead of *"12 conversations"*. Every other tab is one row per
  ticket.
</Callout>

---

## Mark a Contact as Spam

Two routes, same result:

| From | How |
|------|-----|
| **The Close menu** | Open a ticket → **Close** → **Mark contact as spam**. On an already-closed ticket it sits in the **⋮** overflow menu |
| **Ticket Details panel** | Open the panel → **Mark contact as spam** at the bottom |

You are asked to confirm — *"Mark Priya Sharma as spam"* — with a warning that their open tickets
will be closed. Click **Mark as spam**.

<ScreenShot src="/docs/screenshots/inbox/mark-as-spam.png" alt="Mark contact as spam, below the close statuses in the Close menu" />

## What Happens

- **Every open ticket** for that contact is closed, with the close reason **Spam**
- All assignees are cleared
- The contact disappears from **Open**, **New**, **Closed**, **All**, **Mine**, **@Mentions** and
  **Favourites**, and stops counting towards their badges
- They appear once in **Spam**, with a red **Spam** pill on the row
- A system note records who flagged them and when
- The composer is replaced by a banner: *"This contact is marked as spam."*

New messages from a spam contact still arrive — WhatsApp is not told anything — but they land on
that contact's row in **Spam** rather than in **New**.

<Callout type="info">
  Because the flag is on the contact, there is no **Close** or **Reopen** on a spam ticket, and
  selection mode in the Spam tab offers no bulk assign or close. Only **Not spam** moves the contact
  forward.
</Callout>

---

## Take a Contact Back Out of Spam

<Steps>
<Step>
Open the contact from the **Spam** tab.
</Step>
<Step>
Click **Not spam** — in the chat header, in the banner above the composer, or at the bottom of the
**Ticket Details** panel.
</Step>
<Step>
Confirm. The flag is cleared and the contact returns to your working views.
</Step>
</Steps>

Their old tickets stay closed — they were genuinely finished. The contact's next message opens a
fresh ticket in **New**.

## Spam vs Blocking

Both apply to the contact. The difference is whether the messages still reach you.

| | Mark contact as spam | Block contact |
|---|---------------------|---------------|
| **Scope** | The contact, and all their tickets | The contact |
| **Effect** | Open tickets close; the contact moves to **Spam** | Neither side can message the other |
| **Future messages** | Still arrive, into the **Spam** tab | Do not arrive at all |
| **Reversible** | Yes, with **Not spam** | Yes, by unblocking |

To block: open a ticket, use the **Close** menu — or the **⋮** overflow menu if the ticket is
already closed — and choose **Block contact**. You will be asked to confirm.

<Callout type="warn">
  Blocking cuts communication in both directions. Use it for repeat offenders; use spam for noise
  you simply do not want in the queue.
</Callout>

A contact who is both spam **and** blocked still appears in **Blocked**, so they never vanish
entirely.

## Clearing Out Spam for Good

Admins can permanently delete spam conversations, one at a time from the **⋮** menu or in bulk:
**Select Chats**, tick the rows or use select-all, then **Delete selected chats**. The contact stays
flagged as spam, so anything they send afterwards still lands here.

<Callout type="warn">
  Deleting cannot be undone. Only delete spam you are sure you will never need as evidence — abuse
  reports and chargeback disputes sometimes do.
</Callout>

[Deleting in detail →](/inbox/conversation-lifecycle#deleting-a-conversation)

## Best Practices

- Use **Spam** for people, and the close statuses for conversations that are finished
- Review the tab occasionally — genuine customers do end up here by accident
- Block only when flagging as spam is not enough
