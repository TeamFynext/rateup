The RateUp Inbox is organised around **tickets**. Everything else in this section — views,
assignment, closing, reporting — is built on this one idea, so it is worth reading properly.

---

## What Is a Ticket?

A ticket is **one topic with one customer**, from the first message about it until it is closed.

Each ticket has:

| | |
|---|---|
| **Number** | `#1042` — unique in your organisation, never reused |
| **Title** | A short summary of the topic |
| **Description** | Optional longer context |
| **Owner** | One agent, several agents, or a team |
| **Status** | New, Assigned or Closed |
| **Result** | How it ended, if it was closed with an outcome |
| **Messages** | Only the messages that belong to this topic |

---

## One Customer, Several Tickets

A customer can have any number of tickets, and several can be open at once.

<ScreenShot src="/docs/screenshots/inbox/tickets-same-contact.png" alt="Three tickets for the same customer, each its own row" />

Each ticket:

- Appears as its **own row** in the Inbox
- Has its **own status, owner and result**
- Shows **only its own messages** when you open it

<Callout type="info">
  Opening a ticket shows that ticket's messages only. You will never see a different topic's
  messages mixed into the thread — but the customer's history is always one click away.
</Callout>

---

## Why It Works This Way

Before tickets, the Inbox showed one row per **customer**, so every message a person had ever sent
lived in one endless thread. That made three things hard:

| Problem | How tickets fix it |
|---------|--------------------|
| A resolved issue reopened when the customer asked something unrelated | The new question opens a new ticket; the old one stays closed |
| Two agents worked on different issues in one thread | Each issue has its own owner |
| "How long did that take to resolve?" had no answer | Each ticket has its own created, closed and result data |

## Ticket Status

The status pill appears on the row, in the chat header, and in the history bar — always the same
three values:

| Status | Meaning | When it changes |
|--------|---------|-----------------|
| **New** | Nobody is assigned yet | The moment someone is assigned |
| **Assigned** | At least one agent or team owns it | When it is closed, or all assignees are removed |
| **Closed** | Closed, resolved or unresolved | When it is reopened — as a new ticket |

<Callout type="info">
  Status is worked out automatically from assignment and closure. There is no status field to keep
  in sync by hand.
</Callout>

## Ticket History

When a customer has more than one ticket, an amber bar appears under the chat header:

> 🕘 **3 previous conversations** · last on Mar 12, 2026

<ScreenShot src="/docs/screenshots/inbox/previous-conversations.png" alt="Expanded ticket history for a returning customer" />

Click the bar to expand it. Each earlier ticket shows:

- Channel icon
- Ticket number and title
- Status pill
- Assigned agents
- Close result, if it had one
- Creation date

Click any entry to open that ticket. Use this before replying to a returning customer — it is the
fastest way to see what your team already told them.

## Editing a Ticket's Title & Description

Titles matter: they are what your team reads in the conversation list.

<Steps>
<Step>
Open the ticket and click the **panel icon** in the chat header.
</Step>
<Step>
Scroll to **Title & Description** and click the **pencil** icon.
</Step>
<Step>
Write a short, specific title — *"Refund not received for order 8821"* beats *"Question"*.
</Step>
<Step>
Add a description if there is context the messages do not make obvious, then click **Save**.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Callout type="info">
  The title and description belong to **this ticket only**. Contact details — name, phone, labels,
  lifecycle — are shared across all of that customer's tickets.
</Callout>

## The Life of a Ticket

<Steps>
<Step>
**A customer writes in.** A ticket is created with status **New** and appears in the **New** and
**Open** views.
</Step>
<Step>
**Someone takes ownership.** Status becomes **Assigned**; the ticket appears in that person's
**Mine** view. Replying is only possible once a ticket has an owner.
</Step>
<Step>
**The team works on it.** Replies go to the customer; private notes and @mentions stay internal.
</Step>
<Step>
**It is closed with an outcome** — resolved, closed or unresolved. Assignees are cleared and the
ticket moves to **Closed**.
</Step>
<Step>
**The customer comes back.** A new ticket is created. The closed one keeps its record and shows up
in the history bar.
</Step>
</Steps>

## Frequently Asked

<Accordions type="single">
<Accordion title="A customer replied to a closed ticket. Where did their message go?">
Into a **new ticket**, which appears in your **New** view. The closed ticket stays closed so its
resolution time and outcome are not distorted.
</Accordion>
<Accordion title="Can I merge two tickets?">
Not directly. If a customer opened two tickets about the same thing, close one with the result
**Duplicate** and continue on the other.
</Accordion>
<Accordion title="Do ticket numbers ever repeat?">
No. Numbers are unique within your organisation and are not reused, even after a ticket is closed.
</Accordion>
<Accordion title="Can I reopen a ticket exactly as it was?">
Clicking **Reopen** opens a *successor* ticket and carries over the people who were assigned when
it closed. The original stays closed as a permanent record of what happened.
</Accordion>
</Accordions>
