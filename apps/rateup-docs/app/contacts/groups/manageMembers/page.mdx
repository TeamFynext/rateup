# Manage Group Members

The **Manage Members** section explains how to add, remove, and control contacts inside a group. It focuses specifically on member-level actions within a group.

## Adding Members to a Group

You can add contacts to a group at any time using the **Add Members** option available in Group Details.

  src={`${CDN}/docs/screenshots/contacts/add-members-modal.png`}

/>

### Ways to Add Members

You can add members by:

- **Adding a new contact**  
  Create a new contact and directly add it to the group.

- **Selecting existing contacts**  
  Choose from contacts already available in your contact list.

- **Importing contacts**  
  Upload contacts using **CSV** or **Excel** files.

---

## Member Information During Add

While adding members, you may be able to set additional information such as:

- Labels  
- Lifecycle stage  
- Assignee  
- Custom fields (for example, coupon)  
- Internal notes  

This helps ensure contacts are fully enriched when added to the group.

---

## Viewing Group Members

Once added, members appear in the **Group Members List**.

Each member row displays contact details such as:

- Name and avatar  
- Phone / IGSID  
- Labels (if any)  
- Source  
- Date added to the group  

This list helps track who belongs to the group.

---

## Removing Members from a Group

Each member row includes a **Remove member** action.

- Click **Remove member** to take the contact out of the group
- Removing a member does **not delete** the contact from the system

This ensures contacts remain available for use in other groups or conversations.

---

## Best Practices for Managing Members

- Keep group membership up to date  
- Use labels and lifecycle stages for better segmentation  
- Avoid adding unnecessary contacts to large groups  
- Review group members periodically  

---

## Why Managing Members Matters

Proper member management helps teams:

- Maintain clean and accurate groups  
- Improve targeting and segmentation  
- Avoid duplicate or outdated group members  
- Work more efficiently with large contact sets
