Everything about one lead lives in the **lead window** that opens over the board. Open it by
clicking a card on the [Board](/pipelines/board), or a row in the [List](/pipelines/list).

<ScreenShot src="/docs/screenshots/pipelines/pipelines-lead.png" alt="The lead window: header actions, stage stepper with likelihood and idle time, Details panel and Notes tab" />

This page describes a lead on the Leads board. Tickets, tasks and cards on other board types open in
the same window with a few differences — see
[Board Types](/pipelines/board-types#cards-on-support-projects-and-custom-boards).

---

## Adding a Lead

**Add lead** in the header opens on no particular column; the **+** on a column header opens on that
one.

<Steps>
<Step>
Pick the **Customer**, either way round. **Choose from contacts** searches your existing
[contacts](/contacts) by name, phone or email; **Enter details manually** takes a **Contact name**,
an optional **Business / company** and an optional **WhatsApp number** for somebody who is not in
your contacts yet.
</Step>
<Step>
Pick the **Stage** it starts in.
</Step>
<Step>
Pick the **Source** — **WhatsApp enquiry**, **Instagram DM**, **Messenger**, **Web survey**,
**Referral program**, **Loyalty program** or **Added manually**. The first three also set the
channel the lead is reachable on.
</Step>
<Step>
Optionally tap the **Assignees** you want on it, then **Add lead**.
</Step>
</Steps>

If the customer already has a lead on this board, the dialog stays open and says so — *"That
customer is already on this board."* Pick a different customer, or find their existing card.

The lead copies the contact's name, phone and email so a card can render without a second lookup,
but it is the contact link that matters — it is what makes **Open conversation** work.

A number typed in manually is looked up as you finish it, and the line under the field says what
will happen with it:

| It says | Meaning |
|---------|---------|
| *Nobody has this number yet — it will be added to your contacts* | A new contact is created with the lead |
| *{`{name}`} already has this number — the lead will be linked to them* | **Use their details** links the lead to that contact instead of duplicating them |
| *Linked to {`{name}`} — the lead opens their conversation in Inbox* | Already linked. **Unlink** breaks it |

A name on its own is enough to save. That lead has no contact behind it, so its card carries the
broken-link icon and **Open conversation** stays disabled until somebody gives it a number.
A number that is typed has to be dialable — *"That number doesn't look right."* refuses anything
else, since the lead carries it verbatim.

<Callout type="info">
  Choosing a closing column says so: *"{`{stage}`} is a closing column — the lead opens already
  closed."* That is occasionally what you want, for a deal you are recording after the fact.
</Callout>

## The Header

| Button | Does |
|--------|------|
| **Open conversation** | Jumps to this contact in the [Inbox](/inbox). Disabled on a lead with no contact |
| **Won** | Opens the close confirmation. Disabled on a lead already won |
| **Lost** | The same, asking for a reason. Disabled on a lead already lost |
| Link icon | **Copy link to this card**: a short link that opens the board with this lead's window on top. Teammates still need to sign in, and to be on one of the board's teams. On a phone it opens the share sheet instead |
| **⋯ → Reopen lead** | Puts a closed lead back on the board. Disabled while it is open |
| **⋯ → Delete lead** | Deletes the lead after a confirmation. Only shown to people whose role can **Delete boards or move them between teams** — Owners and Admins by default |

<Callout type="warn">
  Deleting a lead takes its notes, tasks and history with it, and cannot be undone. If the lead was
  real but did not work out, mark it **Lost** instead — a lost lead stays on the board and still
  counts in your [insights](/pipelines/insights).
</Callout>

Under the header sits the **stage stepper**: every **open** column in order, with the current one
highlighted. Clicking another step moves the lead there; on a closed lead it reads
**Reopen in {`{stage}`}** and puts the lead back on the board in that column. Under the steps, one
line sums the lead up: its win probability (**{`{n}`}% likely**), days in the current column, days
idle — **idle {`{n}`}d — stalled** once past the column's rotting window — and its age.

## Next Activities

The single most useful thing on a lead: what happens next, and when.

<Steps>
<Step>
**Schedule**, under the details, opens the form. Type **What happens next?** — *"Follow up on the
quote"*.
</Step>
<Step>
Pick a **Type**: **Call**, **Message**, **Meeting**, **Document** or **To-do**. The type only
chooses the icon that shows on the card.
</Step>
<Step>
Set a **Due** date and time — it starts at this time tomorrow. Then **Schedule**.
</Step>
</Steps>

A lead can hold several. They list under **Next activities ({`{n}`})**, each with its type, due date
and the lead's assignees, a **Mark done** button and a bin to remove it. **+ Schedule** adds
another. The earliest one is what the board card shows.

An overdue activity turns red on the card, in the list and in the window. A lead with nothing
scheduled shows **No next activity scheduled** in amber — *"Leads without a next step stall —
schedule one now."* — and appears under the sidebar's **No next activity** view.

Closing a lead marks all of its open activities done.

<Callout type="info">
  *Leads without a next step stall.* The **No next activity** count is the one number on this screen
  you can act on immediately — every lead in it is a decision nobody has made.
</Callout>

## Details

| Field | Notes |
|-------|-------|
| **Assignees** | Everyone working the lead, and the one field you can change here. Pick from the people on the board's teams; **Unassign everyone** clears it |
| **Owner** | Whoever opened it, ringed in amber. Fixed, and shown as **Added automatically** for a lead a rule opened |
| **Source** and **Channel** | How it arrived, and where the customer is reachable |
| **Expected close** | Feeds the [Forecast](/pipelines/forecast). **Not set** puts it in the unscheduled bucket. Read-only here: the lead window has no date picker for it on a Leads board, so it is set by the **Expected close** column of an [import](/pipelines/import-export) |
| **Contact** | The linked contact, or **Not linked** in amber |

The lead's age and idle time are on the line under the stage stepper.

<Callout type="info">
  Labels are the board's own and are separate from [contact labels](/contacts/labels). They show as
  chips on a board card, but the lead window has no label picker and pipeline settings have no label
  editor, so on a Leads board there is currently no way to put one on a lead. Nothing else on the
  lead depends on labels.
</Callout>

## Notes and Activity

The right-hand side of the window has two tabs.

**Notes ({`{n}`})** is what your team writes down. Type in the box and press **Enter** to save, or
**Shift+Enter** for a new line. Above the notes, **Last activity** shows the latest entry from the
other tab; click it to jump there.

| On a note | Who |
|-----------|-----|
| Author, time, and **edited** if it was changed | Everyone |
| **Edit note** | Only the person who wrote it |
| **Delete note** | Anyone who can open the lead, after a confirmation |

**Activity** is the automatic record — created, moved, assignees changed, won, lost, reopened, note
added, next activity scheduled. Each entry names who did it, or says it was added by automation when
a rule did. On a board synced with a CRM, entries also say whether the change reached it, such as
*"Synced to {`{crm}`}, but not the stage"* or *"Not sent to {`{crm}`}"*.

<Callout type="info">
  Notes count as activity, so leaving one resets the idle clock and clears a **needs attention**
  flag. That is intended — someone has looked at it — but it does mean a lead can be kept out of the
  stalled list without moving forward.
</Callout>

## Closing a Lead

Closing happens two ways, and both end in the same confirmation: **Won** or **Lost** in the lead
window's header, or dragging the card onto a closing column on the [Board](/pipelines/board). The
stage stepper only lists open columns, so it cannot close a lead.

| Outcome | What it asks |
|---------|--------------|
| **Won** | Confirmation only. *"The lead moves to the won column and stops counting as open."* |
| **Lost** | A **Lost reason**, picked from the board's list — and it is required when the board has any |

Both accept an optional closing note, which is kept on the **Activity** tab. Closing also marks
every open next activity on the lead done.

<Callout type="warn">
  The lost reason is the only input the *"why do we lose deals"* side of reporting ever gets. A board
  with no lost reasons configured lets a loss through without one, and that loss can never be
  attributed afterwards — add them in [Settings](/pipelines/settings) before your team starts closing.
</Callout>

A closed lead stays on the board in its closing column, stops counting as open, and can be put back
with **Reopen lead**.

## Troubleshooting

| Symptom | Cause | Fix |
|---------|-------|-----|
| **Open conversation** is greyed out | The lead has no linked contact | It was created without one; raise a new lead from the contact |
| Nobody appears in the assignee picker | Nobody is on the board's teams yet | Add a team in [Settings](/pipelines/settings) |
| **Mark lost** stays disabled | The board has lost reasons but none is selected | Pick one |
| No **Delete lead** in the **⋯** menu | Your role cannot delete | Ask an Owner or Admin, or mark it **Lost** |
| *That customer is already on this board.* | A contact holds one lead per board | Open their existing card |
| **Edit note** is missing on a note | You did not write it | Ask its author, or delete it and write your own |
